## Findings
1. **The decline started in May, not on 10 Oct.** Booking.com went from 13–23 bookings a month in March–April to 0–3 a month from June. It is a 5-month slide, so look for something that changed around May (ranking, review score, promotions, content, a price or availability setting), not a recent outage.
2. **It is not price.** What Booking.com guests paid fell over the same period (£195 in May bookings → £118–131 in Sep–Oct), so the property did not get more expensive for guests while volume fell.
3. **Airbnb only partly filled the gap.** Airbnb bookings on the flat (both listings) were 1–4 a month in June–October. October stays: Airbnb 22 nights, Booking.com 4. November has 11 booked nights (all Airbnb), December 5.
4. **No future Booking.com stays at all.** Booking.com guests here book late (median 7.5 days before arrival), so this matters most for the next 2 weeks: 38 of the next 60 nights are open (22 Oct – 20 Dec), at a PriceLabs average of £223, which Booking.com shows as about £268 before promotions.
5. **Early bookers cancel.** 16 of 34 bookings made more than 30 days ahead were cancelled (47%), against 1 of 24 made within a week (4%). Overall 21 of 67 (31%). The non-refundable rate exists (−10%), so guests are choosing the flexible rate.
6. **Min stay is not the blocker.** Open nights carry a 2-night minimum (22 nights), 3 nights (14) or 1 night (2).
