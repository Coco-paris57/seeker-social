## How to read this document

| Tag | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **[confirmed]** | Date verified against the venue's or organiser's own website, or an official governing-body listing, in September 2026. Assume it holds. |
| **[unconfirmed]** | Recurring event whose 2026/27 date is not yet published, or a date taken from a secondary source. Pattern-based estimate — **do not put it in a paid ad or a guest email without re-checking**. |
| **[TV-movable]** | Football/rugby fixture that is scheduled but may shift by a day or by kick-off time for broadcast. Book the content, not the exact hour. |

**Two accuracy warnings up front:**

1. **Premier League and domestic-cup fixtures are deliberately under-specified in this document.** Arsenal, Chelsea, Tottenham, West Ham, Crystal Palace, Fulham and Brentford collectively play a home game in London on essentially every weekend in this window, but individual dates move for TV with ~5 weeks' notice. The reliable planning unit is the *matchweek*, not the *match*. Confirmed structural dates (Wembley finals, England internationals, Champions League matchdays) are given precisely.
2. **The Nitto ATP Finals are NOT in London.** They are in Turin, 15–22 November 2026. London's equivalent tennis moment this autumn is the **Laver Cup at The O2, 25–27 September 2026**. Do not build content on an ATP Finals that isn't happening here.

### The headline conclusions

**Highest-demand blocks (defend rate, tighten minimum stays, no discounting):**
1. **12–18 October 2026 — Frieze Week.** The peak.
2. **16 November – 13 December 2026** — an almost unbroken four-week peak: rugby internationals, Winter Wonderland opening, Nations Championship finals, and corporate Christmas parties stacked on top of each other. This is where the money is.
3. **14–27 September 2026** — Fashion Week / Design Festival / Laver Cup.
4. **30 December 2026 – 1 January 2027** — short, sharp, highest nightly rate.

**Lowest-demand blocks (this is what the content calendar exists to fix):**
1. **4–24 January 2027** — the trough. Three genuinely weak weeks. Content strategy here should not be "come to London for an event", because there aren't many; it should be **"January in London is cheap, quiet, and the museums are empty"** — Veganuary, Dry January, gallery-hopping, the January sales, Winter Lights, and *value* messaging. Push 7+ night discounts, target relocators, contractors, and people between homes.
2. **19–23 October 2026** — the post-Frieze midweek air pocket, before half term.
3. **21–30 December 2026 for corporate stock specifically** — Docklands/City units will be empty. Reposition them as family/VFR Christmas lets with festive photography, not as business apartments.
4. **First week of February 2027** — soft, but rescued by CNY and the Six Nations opening. Lean hard on both.

## JANUARY 2027 — the trough. This is the month the content calendar exists for.

**Strategic note:** 4–24 January is the weakest three-week block in the London short-let year. There is no event that will fix it. The correct content strategy is not "come for X" — it is **"January is when London is cheapest, quietest and most liveable"**, aimed at four specific segments: (1) value-seeking domestic city-breakers, (2) relocators and people between homes, (3) contractors and project-based corporate stays, and (4) long-stay bookers who will take a 21–28 night discount. *Standing content format:* **"Sunday in [neighbourhood]"** — one per property, filmed once, reused seasonally. It is the highest-converting content a short-let operator can make, because it sells the thing guests are actually choosing between (the area), not the thing they can already see in the listing photos (the flat).

### The "what's on this weekend" anchor

Publish **every Thursday morning**, without exception. Format: three to five things happening in London this weekend, at least one free, at least one within a mile of a Seeker property. This is the single highest-value recurring post for a London accommodation account — it builds a habit, it earns saves and shares, and it positions the brand as a local guide rather than a landlord. It also gives you a permanent slot to seed availability without it reading as an ad.

### Seasonal restaurant and openings content

London's restaurant openings cluster in **September/October** and **February/March** — the two "new season" windows. Both fall inside this calendar.
*Formats:* (1) "Opened this month within walking distance"; (2) an honest "we tried it so you don't have to"; (3) the neighbourhood classic that isn't new but is better — a format that ages far better than chasing openings.

### Other evergreen recurring anchors

- **Royal Parks through the seasons** — Richmond deer in autumn rut (Sep–Oct), Kew autumn colour, frost on Hampstead Heath, the first Hyde Park daffodils in late Feb/March.
- **Free museum lates** — the V&A (last Friday of the month), the British Museum, the Science Museum, the Natural History Museum. Under-marketed, free, adult-oriented.
- **Sunrise and sunset content** — Primrose Hill, Greenwich Park, Parliament Hill, Alexandra Palace, the Sky Garden (free, EC3, must be pre-booked). Peak content value between October and February when sunset falls at a sociable hour.
- **Tube and transport content** — night Tube lines, the Elizabeth line, the fact that the IFS Cloud Cable Car takes a contactless tap. Consistently high-performing and genuinely useful.
- **Weather content** — the first frost, the fog on the Thames, snow (rare, and enormous engagement when it happens). Keep a snow post drafted and ready.
- **Countdowns to the big ones** — Frieze, Winter Wonderland opening, the darts, NYE, the Six Nations. A 30-day, 7-day and day-of post for each of the top 25 events.

# HOW TO OPERATIONALISE THIS

### Content cadence

| Slot | Frequency | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **"What's on this weekend"** | Every Thursday | Habit-forming anchor; the backbone of the feed |
| **Event countdown (30/7/0 days)** | For each of the top 25 events | Converts intent into bookings |
| **Neighbourhood/market content** | 2× per week | Sells the area; works when there's no event |
| **Corporate "who's in town"** | Monthly, LinkedIn | ExCeL/Olympia/BDC shows for next month — highest corporate ROI |
| **Seasonal/urgency ("closing soon")** | 2× per month | Especially valuable in the January trough |
| **Availability/rate post** | Sparingly — after value, never before | 1 in 8 posts maximum |

### The lead-time rule

Every event in this calendar has a **booking lead time**, and posting on the day is posting too late.

| Segment | Lead time | Post at |
|---|---|---|
| Family holidays (half terms, Easter, Christmas) | 8–14 weeks | 8 weeks minimum |
| International sport (NFL, rugby internationals, Six Nations) | 10–20 weeks | 12 weeks |
| Frieze / Fashion Week / trade congresses | 6–10 weeks | 8 weeks |
| Arena concerts | 3–8 weeks | 6 weeks |
| Corporate/trade shows | 2–6 weeks | 4 weeks |
| NYE | 6–10 weeks (plus a 48-hour last-minute surge) | 8 weeks, then again on 30 Dec |
| Football (TV-movable) | 4–5 weeks (when fixtures confirm) | The day the fixture is confirmed |

### Five things to verify before you publish anything paid

1. **February 2027 London Fashion Week dates** — British Fashion Council, from ~November.
2. **2026 Christmas lights switch-on dates and borough Bonfire Night displays** — from ~early October.
3. **Royal Albert Hall December programme** — site was inaccessible during this research; check directly.
4. **Chinese New Year parade date (Sun 7 Feb 2027?)** and **St Patrick's Day parade date (Sun 14 Mar 2027?)** — Mayor of London / london.gov.uk, from ~December and ~January respectively.
5. **Olympia, Business Design Centre and QEII Centre 2027 calendars** — all incomplete at time of research; re-check from November. March 2027 corporate demand is almost certainly understated in this document.
